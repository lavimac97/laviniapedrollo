About

I am a PhD candidate in Civil & Environmental Engineering at Stanford University, advised by Martin Fischer. My research sits between structural engineering, building information modelling and machine learning: can a learned system propose complete, verified support assemblies for a building cross-section, and how do we know when it has?

Before and alongside the thesis I have worked on computer vision for sports tracking, on metrics for mechanical, electrical and plumbing (MEP) design across project phases, and on generative models for building data. Everything I build is checked against an exact verifier or a measured dataset, and I report mixed results as mixed.

Research

PhD thesis

Learning to recommend sets of diverse, verified MEP support assembly designs

Supports for pipes, ducts and cable trays are designed at the cross-section of a corridor, one hanger at a time. The thesis asks whether a learned sequential designer can build such assemblies action by action, avoid partial designs that no completion can rescue, and return a set of distinct verified designs within a fixed compute budget.

  • A grammar that assembles catalog parts step by step, judged only by an exact verifier covering statics, deflection, buildability, geometry and anchoring, cross-checked against OpenSees.
  • SSA-Bench: a versioned benchmark of 196 cross-sections on seven difficulty levels and two catalogs, with equal wall-clock budgets and installed cost from a published labour model.
  • Certified feasibility labels for partial designs, a critic trained on them, and a design-set track measuring how many distinct verified designs each method returns.
  • Imitation learning, reinforcement learning (RLOO, PPO), GFlowNets, tree search and exact CP-SAT search compared on the same footing.

Open dataset · CIB W78 2026

CrossMEP: 7,000 generated MEP cross-sections

A method that wants to learn support design needs thousands of cross-sections to practise on; real projects give a handful, under NDA, all alike. CrossMEP generates them: 7,000 contexts in eight tiers, every number traced to EN, IEC, ASME and SMACNA tables, with a seeded generator reproducible to the byte and an evaluation harness with paired tests.

Computer vision · Stanford

Player tracking across sports through meta-learning

With Akayla Hackson and Miguel Gerena. We integrated Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning with the OSNet re-identification model inside the Deep-EIoU tracker, so it adapts quickly to a new sport with little data. The OSNet-MAML model improved HOTA, MOTA and IDF1 over the base tracker on SportsMOT.

Web tool

MEP Metrics Explorer

An interactive table of metrics for mechanical, electrical and plumbing design: what each measures, what it indicates, and the project phase it belongs to, from feasibility to post-occupancy evaluation, with a bibliography and per-category explanations.
