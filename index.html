About

Engineer, researcher, artist.

I am a PhD candidate in Civil & Environmental Engineering at Stanford University, advised by Martin Fischer. My research sits between structural engineering, building information modelling and machine learning: can a learned system propose complete, verified support assemblies for a building cross-section, and how do we know when it has?

Before and alongside the thesis I have worked on computer vision for sports tracking, on metrics for mechanical, electrical and plumbing (MEP) design across project phases, and on generative models for building data. Everything I build is checked against an exact verifier or a measured dataset, and I report mixed results as mixed.

PhD thesis

Learning to recommend sets of diverse, verified MEP support assembly designs

Supports for pipes, ducts and cable trays are designed at the cross-section of a corridor, one hanger at a time. The thesis asks whether a learned sequential designer can build such assemblies action by action, avoid partial designs that no completion can rescue, and return a set of distinct verified designs within a fixed compute budget.

  • A grammar that assembles catalog parts step by step, judged only by an exact verifier, cross-checked against OpenSees.
  • SSA-Bench: 196 cross-sections on seven levels and two catalogs, equal wall-clock budgets, installed cost from a published labour model.
  • Certified feasibility labels for partial designs, a critic trained on them, and a design-set track that counts distinct verified designs.
  • Imitation learning, RLOO, PPO, GFlowNets, tree search and exact CP-SAT search compared on the same footing.

Open dataset · CIB W78 2026

CrossMEP: 7,000 generated MEP cross-sections

A method that wants to learn support design needs thousands of cross-sections to practise on; real projects give a handful, under NDA, all alike. CrossMEP generates them: eight difficulty tiers, every number traced to EN, IEC, ASME and SMACNA tables, a seeded generator reproducible to the byte, and an evaluation harness with paired tests.

Computer vision · Stanford

Player tracking across sports through meta-learning

With Akayla Hackson and Miguel Gerena. Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning wrapped around the OSNet re-identification model inside the Deep-EIoU tracker, so it adapts to a new sport with little data. Improved HOTA, MOTA and IDF1 over the base tracker on SportsMOT.
